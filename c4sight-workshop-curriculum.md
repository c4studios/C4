# C4Sight workshop curriculum

In-person AI literacy training for Australian workplaces. Half-day and full-day formats. Common core, sector-specific swaps.

This document is the source of truth for delivery, and feeds the "what you'll learn" content on the C4 website.

---

## Design principles

1. Hands-on inside the first hour. Nobody sits through a morning of slides.
2. Teach through the attendee's real work, not abstract examples.
3. The governance module runs every session, no exceptions. It is both curriculum and liability shield.
4. Depth over breadth. Anchor on one or two tools the attendees can actually access, not a tour of ten.
5. Everyone leaves with something they keep: a prompt pack, a workflow, a reference sheet.

---

## Half-day run sheet (3.5 hours, including breaks)

### Block 0 — Setup and framing (15 min)
- Confirm everyone has device, wifi, tool access (checklist was sent pre-session, this is the catch-all)
- Frame the session: by the end you will have done real work with these tools, not just heard about them
- Quick room read: one sentence each on what they currently use AI for, if anything. Sets the level.

### Block 1 — What these tools actually are (30 min)
- Plain-language explanation of large language models: prediction, not knowledge retrieval
- What they are genuinely good at: drafting, summarising, restructuring, explaining, first passes
- Where they confidently fail: fabricated facts and citations, false confidence, no real-time truth, no memory of your specifics unless told
- The single most important mental model: it is an eager junior assistant who never says "I don't know". You are the editor, always.
- No live tool use yet. This is the 30 minutes that makes the rest land.

### Block 2 — First hands-on contact (45 min)
- Everyone opens the tool and does one real task from their own work
- Facilitator-guided, same task type across the room, individual content
- Goal: remove the fear, get a small genuine win each
- Debrief: what surprised you, what went wrong, who got a bad answer (there will be some, this is the teaching gold)

### Break (15 min)

### Block 3 — Prompting that actually works (45 min)
- Taught through their tasks from Block 2, improving them live
- The four levers: context (tell it who and what for), examples (show the output you want), specificity (constraints, format, length), iteration (it is a conversation, not a vending machine)
- Before/after: take a weak prompt from the room, improve it together, see the difference
- Each attendee rewrites one of their own prompts and keeps it

### Block 4 — The governance and safety module (30 min) [NON-NEGOTIABLE]
- What must not go into these tools: confidential information, personal/client data, anything legally privileged, and for schools, any student information
- Why: data handling, privacy obligations, and the fact that inputs may not stay private
- The verification habit: never rely on a factual claim, number, citation, or name without checking it against a real source
- Respecting your own organisation's AI policy: if your workplace bans a tool or restricts use, that overrides anything taught here
- Output framing: you teach methods; no AI tool's output is warranted accurate. The human signs off, every time.
- This block is delivered every session and is the content C4Sight points to as its safety standard.

### Block 5 — Embedding it past Friday (20 min)
- The trap: everyone is excited in the room and forgets by next week
- Pick one or two recurring tasks to actually apply this to
- Build it into an existing habit rather than a new one
- Hand out the prompt pack and reference sheet
- Close: where to go next, what the full-day covers if they want to go deeper

## Full-day extension (additional ~2.5 hours after lunch)

The morning is the half-day, unchanged. The afternoon is role-specific deep work, which is where the real value and margin sit.

### Block 6 — Bring your own task (60 min)
- Attendees bring a genuine recurring task from their role
- Facilitator works the room, building real prompts and mini-workflows against actual work
- This is consulting delivered to a group, and it is what they will remember

### Break (15 min)

### Block 7 — Building a small repeatable workflow (45 min)
- Move from one-off prompts to a saved, reusable process
- Example shapes: a templated drafting prompt, a summarise-then-check routine, a document-review checklist with AI assist
- Each attendee leaves with one workflow documented in their own words

### Block 8 — Role-specific Q&A and edge cases (30 min)
- Open floor on their real situations
- This is where sector risk content gets specific and personal
- Capture common questions; they feed marketing content and future sessions

### Close — Reference pack handout (15 min)
- Consolidated prompt pack, the governance one-pager, the workflows built, where to learn more

## Sector module swaps

The spine above is constant. These three layers change per audience.

### Office workers / general business
- **Example tasks:** emails and replies, document drafting, meeting summaries, spreadsheet formulas and explanations, turning notes into structured docs
- **Risk emphasis:** company confidential information, client data, commercial-in-confidence material
- **Tone:** practical productivity, time saved on the boring parts
- **Win to aim for:** a task they do weekly, done in a third of the time

### Teachers / schools [HIGH CARE]
- **Example tasks:** lesson and unit planning, generating differentiated material for different levels, drafting feedback, creating practice questions, summarising curriculum docs
- **Risk emphasis:** student data and privacy (never input student names or identifying information), age-appropriateness of tool use, academic integrity, and the school's and Department's own policies
- **Tone:** careful and ethics-forward. Teachers are rightly cautious; respect it.
- **Hard requirement:** Working With Children Check held before delivery. Governance module expands to cover student-facing implications explicitly.
- **Win to aim for:** a planning task that normally eats their weekend, halved, with no student data involved

### Law firms [HIGH CARE — your differentiator]
- **Example tasks:** research starting points (not authority), first-draft correspondence, restructuring and plain-Englishing documents, summarising long documents, checklists
- **Risk emphasis:** legal professional privilege, client confidentiality, and the well-documented problem of AI fabricating case citations and authorities. Verification is not optional, it is professional survival.
- **Tone:** conservative, verification-heavy, sceptical by design
- **Your edge:** as a JD student you speak their language and understand the conduct obligations. Lean on this hard in delivery and marketing. It is the reason a law firm picks C4Sight over a generic trainer.
- **Hard framing:** nothing taught is legal advice; practitioners remain bound by their conduct rules; no AI output is ever filed or relied on unverified
- **Win to aim for:** a drafting or summarising task sped up, with a verification routine they trust
